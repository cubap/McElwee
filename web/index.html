    How to read a sheet

    Each line under a name is a claim, not a fact. The line is printed with the record that asserts it. Press any line and the exhibit opens that record: its identifier, who generated it, and the date it was written.

    A line with a 2 variants mark is one where the records disagree. Press it and the competing values splay out side by side. The exhibit does not decide between them.

    A block headed Stray fields in the record holds keys that were never part of the catalog's vocabulary — keystrokes left in the store while the old entry form was being tested. The exhibit reproduces them because it does not delete records it did not make, and keeps them off the label proper because they say nothing about the person.

    An empty mounting space means no image is held. Photographs of the markers exist out on the record, but they are other people's work and are not copied here; the exhibit links to them instead.

    What the map is not

    The plot and parcel outlines associated with this cemetery were drawn from modern county assessor records, not from the historical catalog. They show who holds the ground now. They do not show where any grave lies, and no plot plan from the catalog is held here.

    The locality itself is registered with the U.S. Geological Survey as GNIS feature 722098, which is the one identification on this page backed by an outside authority.

    The catalog
